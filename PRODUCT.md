# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters, technical collaborators, hackathon judges, and Web3 or creative-tech teams reviewing Neil Hong's work. They are scanning for evidence of execution quality, taste, technical range, and whether the portfolio itself demonstrates the interaction craft it claims.

## Product Purpose

This portfolio presents Neil Hong as a Web3 and full-stack developer with creative coding strength. Success means visitors can quickly understand the developer profile, inspect proof of work, review awards and experience, and remember the site because the interaction design feels spatial and deliberate.

## Brand Personality

Technical, kinetic, precise. The interface should feel like a developer portfolio with a strong visual point of view, not a generic resume site or template landing page.

## Anti-references

Avoid AI-generated landing-page tropes: repeated tiny section eyebrows, generic equal-card grids, decorative gradients without purpose, over-rounded ghost cards, washed-out gray text, and generic copy. Avoid losing the current 3D project-wheel identity or making motion feel like unrelated decoration.

## Design Principles

1. Interaction is proof. Motion and layout should demonstrate technical ability, not merely describe it.
2. Keep the scan path clear. Recruiters should understand role, skills, projects, and credibility without decoding the design.
3. Use contrast with restraint. The dark visual system and teal accent should stay focused and legible.
4. Make each section earn its shape. Cards, stacks, media, and typography should vary by content purpose.
5. Preserve spatial continuity. Route transitions and scroll effects should feel directional and connected.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for body text and controls. Keep keyboard-visible focus states, descriptive labels for icon-only links, reduced-motion fallbacks for GSAP and CSS animation, and avoid nested scroll regions that trap wheel or touch input.
